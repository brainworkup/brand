**7 directions for brainworkup.org — pediatric & forensic neuropsychology**

## Concept 1 · "Growing Minds" *(Warm & Supportive)*

**Name:** Brainworkup Neuropsychology

**Tagline:** *Understanding every mind at every stage*

**Visual direction:** Soft gradient from teal to warm coral. A logomark built from an abstract brain silhouette where the folds subtly form the profile of a child looking upward. Rounded sans-serif type (think Nunito or Quicksand for "Brainworkup," paired with a clean medical serif for "Neuropsychology"). Favicon: the child-profile-in-brain icon in teal on white.

**Color palette:** Teal (#2A9D8F), warm coral (#E07A5F), soft cream (#FAF3E0), charcoal (#264653)

**Tone:** Reassuring, parent-facing. The website reads like a conversation with a knowledgeable friend who happens to have a PhD. Content emphasizes "we see your child, not just their scores." Legal/forensic services live on a separate, more formal subpage.

**Letterhead:** Clean top bar in teal with the logo left-aligned, Dr. Trampush's credentials in a lighter weight beneath. Warm but unmistakably clinical.

**Best for:** Private clients (parents seeking evaluations), school referrals. Slightly harder sell for forensic/legal audiences who want gravitas.

## Concept 2 · "The Lighthouse" *(Warm & Supportive)*

**Name:** Brainworkup

**Tagline:** *Clarity through assessment*

**Visual direction:** A minimal lighthouse icon where the light beam subtly forms a neural pathway or synapse pattern. The metaphor: navigating uncertainty, finding clarity. Warm navy (#1D3557) and golden amber (#F4A261) as primaries, with white space doing most of the design work. Typography is a humanist sans-serif like Lato or Source Sans — approachable but not childish.

**Color palette:** Navy (#1D3557), amber (#F4A261), soft white (#FEFAE0), slate (#457B9D)

**Tone:** Empathetic and clear. Speaks to worried parents and attorneys alike — both groups looking for someone who can make sense of complexity. Avoids jargon on the homepage, layers it into clinical pages.

**Letterhead:** Navy header band, gold accent line, logo centered. Clean and versatile enough for both a parent intake letter and a forensic report cover page.

**Best for:** Versatile across audiences. The lighthouse metaphor works for parents ("finding answers") and legal ("illuminating the evidence") without forcing a tonal shift.

## Concept 3 · "Clinical Modern" *(Mixed)*

**Name:** Brainworkup Neuropsychology

**Tagline:** *Pediatric & forensic neuropsychological assessment*

**Visual direction:** Minimal geometric brain icon — think 4-5 clean arcs or lines that suggest neural architecture without being literal. Monochromatic with a single accent color. The "B" and "W" in Brainworkup could be subtly emphasized as a secondary mark for the favicon. Typography: Inter or Söhne for body, a slightly heavier weight for the wordmark.

**Color palette:** Deep blue (#1B365D), clinical white (#FFFFFF), accent green (#4CAF50), light gray (#F5F5F5)

**Tone:** Professional, evidence-based, but still human. Think Mayo Clinic meets a well-designed tech company. Content leads with credentials and methodology, but the language stays accessible. Forensic and pediatric services presented as equal pillars.

**Letterhead:** Crisp. Logo top-left, thin blue rule across the page. USC affiliation and board certifications in the footer. This is the letterhead that opposing counsel takes seriously.

**Best for:** Hospital referrals, legal/forensic work, and parents who are Googling "best neuropsychologist in LA" and want to see credentials front and center.

## Concept 4 · "The Institute" *(Mixed — leaning academic)*

**Name:** Brainworkup Institute

**Tagline:** *Neuropsychological science in practice*

**Visual direction:** "Institute" elevates the brand from private practice to something that sounds like it has a research mission (which you do, given your USC position). Logomark: a stylized "BWI" monogram inside a clean shield or circle — institutional but not stuffy. Think Stanford's or Hopkins' department branding. A secondary wordmark reads "Brainworkup Institute for Neuropsychological Assessment."

**Color palette:** Burgundy (#6B2737), gold (#C9A94E), ivory (#FFFDF5), charcoal (#2D2D2D)

**Tone:** Authoritative but warm enough for parents. The word "Institute" signals depth and seriousness. Website could have a "Research & Publications" section that links to your academic work, bridging the clinical-research gap. Forensic pages emphasize "institute-level rigor."

**Letterhead:** Formal. Monogram top-center, full name beneath, gold accent. This is stationery that looks like it belongs in a courtroom exhibit.

**Naming consideration:** "Institute" works if you want to signal that this isn't just a solo practice — it's a center of expertise. It also future-proofs the brand if you ever bring on associates or fellows.

## Concept 5 · "Neural Blueprint" *(Mixed)*

**Name:** Brainworkup

**Tagline:** *Mapping the developing mind*

**Visual direction:** Blueprint/schematic aesthetic. The logo uses thin, precise lines to create an abstract brain cross-section that looks like an architectural drawing — the idea being that neuropsychological assessment is the "blueprint" for understanding a child's cognitive architecture. Monoline icon style. Could work beautifully as a favicon (just the cross-section mark).

**Color palette:** Blueprint blue (#1E3A5F), drafting pencil gray (#6C757D), white (#FFFFFF), warm accent (#E8927C)

**Tone:** Smart and distinctive. Appeals to professionals (attorneys, psychiatrists, pediatricians) who appreciate the precision metaphor. Parents see it as "this person will be thorough." Content balances clinical detail with plain-language summaries.

**Letterhead:** Clean engineering aesthetic. Thin-line border, logo top-left with a subtle grid pattern watermark at very low opacity. Modern and memorable.

**Best for:** Differentiation. This doesn't look like any other neuropsych practice. Stands out in a sea of brain-and-rainbow logos.

## Concept 6 · "Academic Authority" *(Highly Academic & Authoritative)*

**Name:** Brainworkup Institute of Neuropsychology

**Tagline:** *Evidence-based assessment · Expert testimony · Clinical science*

**Visual direction:** Full institutional treatment. A crest or seal incorporating classic academic elements (an open book, a stylized brain, founding year) — but executed in a modern, flat design so it doesn't look like a 19th-century university. Think of how modern law firms or medical schools balance tradition with clean design. Primary wordmark is set in a serif face like Freight or Merriweather.

**Color palette:** Oxford navy (#002147), gold (#D4A843), white, warm gray (#9E9E9E)

**Tone:** Unapologetically academic. Dr. Trampush's CV is a brand asset here — USC faculty, NIH postdoc, 20+ years of clinical experience. The website structure mirrors an academic department: About, Clinical Services, Forensic Services, Research & Publications, Referrals. Language is precise and field-appropriate.

**Letterhead:** Seal top-center. Full credentials. This is the letterhead for forensic reports that get entered into evidence and for letters to judges. Parents who find this practice are the ones who want the most credentialed expert they can find.

**Best for:** Forensic/legal referrals, hospital partnerships, IME work. May feel intimidating to some parent audiences, but that's the tradeoff for maximum authority.

## Concept 7 · "Dual Identity" *(Highly Academic + Warm — strategic hybrid)*

**Name:** Brainworkup Institute *(parent brand)* with two sub-brands:
- **Brainworkup Pediatric** — warm, developmental, family-facing
- **Brainworkup Forensic** — authoritative, evidence-driven, legal-facing

**Tagline (parent):** *Neuropsychological assessment across the lifespan*

**Visual direction:** A unified logomark (abstract brain/neural mark) that works in two color treatments. Pediatric materials use the warm palette (teal, coral, cream); forensic materials use the authoritative palette (navy, gold, white). Same typography, same mark, different temperature. The website has a subtle fork: "I'm a parent seeking an evaluation" vs. "I'm an attorney or professional seeking services."

**Color palettes:**
- Pediatric: Teal (#2A9D8F), coral (#E07A5F), cream (#FAF3E0)
- Forensic: Navy (#1B365D), gold (#C9A94E), ivory (#FFFDF5)
- Shared: Charcoal (#2D2D2D) for body text

**Tone:** This is the "have it both ways" option. A parent landing on the pediatric path sees warmth, developmental language, and reassurance. An attorney landing on the forensic path sees credentials, methodology, and precision. Both paths lead to the same clinician, but the framing matches the audience.

**Letterhead:** Two versions. Same logo, different color headers. Pediatric letters feel approachable; forensic reports feel institutional.

**Best for:** Practices that genuinely serve both audiences and don't want to compromise either. More complex to implement (two sets of materials, two website paths), but maximally effective.

## On the "Institute" Question

Renaming to **Brainworkup Institute** makes sense if:

- You want to signal that this is more than a solo private practice
- You want to leverage your USC faculty appointment and research publications as brand assets
- Forensic/legal work is a significant or growing revenue stream
- You may eventually bring on associates, postdocs, or psychometrists under the brand

Stick with **Brainworkup Neuropsychology** if:

- You want to keep things simple and personal
- Your primary audience is parents who want to feel like they're working with *a person*, not an institution
- You don't want the overhead of a more institutional brand identity

A middle path: **Brainworkup Institute** as the legal/brand entity, but you still introduce yourself as "Dr. Trampush at Brainworkup" in conversation and parent-facing materials.

## On Moving Away from the Data Science Website Aesthetic

Your current Quarto site modeled after a data science site probably has: dark theme options, code-block styling, monospace fonts, and a developer-oriented layout. For a clinical practice, you'd want to shift toward:

- Light, airy backgrounds (cream or white, not dark mode)
- Serif or humanist sans-serif typography (not monospace)
- Hero images of children, families, or abstract neuroscience imagery (not terminal screenshots)
- Clear calls to action ("Schedule an Evaluation," "Referral Information")
- Credential badges near the top (board certification, USC affiliation)
- Testimonial or outcome sections (within HIPAA bounds)

Quarto can still be your build tool — you'd just need a custom theme/template that looks clinical rather than technical. Or this could be a good reason to migrate to something like Squarespace or a custom Hugo/Next.js site that gives you more design control.
